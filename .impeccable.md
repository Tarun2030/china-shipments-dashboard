## Design Context

### Users
Primarily Tarun, checked frequently (desktop and mobile) as a live operational brief. Also shared with colleagues/buyers/partners occasionally, so it needs to read as credible and trustworthy to outside eyes, not just a personal scratchpad. Provenance ("what is this number, where did it come from") and lightweight definitions for domain jargon (FCL, MT, variance) matter for that outside audience — no glossary panel, just inline `<abbr title="…">` at first/prominent use.

### Brand Personality
Terminal / trading-desk brief. Dense, factual, no-nonsense. Data speaks for itself — the UI gets out of the way. Three words: precise, restrained, editorial.

### Aesthetic Direction
Editorial data terminal — warm paper, not brutalist white. This superseded the original near-black-on-white IBM Plex direction; do not deviate without asking first.

- **Typography**: Bodoni Moda (serif display — the single hero figure per view, `--t-display`, and header title) + Schibsted Grotesk (body/UI text) + Spline Sans Mono (every number/figure/label — tabular, `font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums`, so columns of digits align).
- **Type ramp** — 7 tokens, nothing below 11px anywhere, ever:
  `--t-micro:11px` (uppercase tracked labels, the absolute floor) · `--t-sm:12px` (secondary text,
  table cells) · `--t-body:13px` (default) · `--t-lg:17px` (detail values, list IDs) · `--t-xl:24px`
  (KPI values) · `--t-2xl:34px` (panel lead figures) · `--t-display:52px` (the ONE hero figure per
  view — never more than one on screen at a time, even across a pinned rail + a scrollable detail
  pane that are visible simultaneously).
  The bottom three steps are deliberately close in size, mirroring Apple's Caption2/Caption1/Footnote
  (11/12/13): they are separated by case, tracking and family — micro is uppercase tracked mono,
  sm and body are sans — not by size. 11px is a legibility floor, not a style choice.
- **Palette**: warm paper ground (`--paper`/`--paper-2`/`--paper-3`, OKLCH tinted toward the paper
  hue rather than cold grey), ink text (`--ink`/`--ink-2`/`--ink-3`, darkest→lightest), hairline
  rules (`--rule`/`--rule-strong`). Every text colour must hit 4.5:1 (WCAG AA) against `--paper-2`,
  the harshest surface in use (the pinned KPI rail) — this is enforced by the verification harness,
  not just eyeballed. `--accent` (terracotta) is reserved for exactly the one hero figure per view
  plus row selection; it is never decorative. Status colour (`--green`/`--amber`/`--red`/`--blue`) is
  never the only signal — every status also carries a distinct dot silhouette (ring / diamond /
  triangle / square / filled circle) and a text label, so state survives greyscale or colour-blindness.
- **No boxes.** Hierarchy comes from type scale, ink-shade, and whitespace/hairlines — not cards,
  borders, or shadows. A bordered/boxed treatment needs a specific, current justification (see
  `.pmt-panel` in the stylesheet for the one deliberate exception and why it earns a hairline rule
  instead of a full box). Sharp corners throughout; no gradients, no glassmorphism, no drop shadows.
- **Layout is a non-scrolling cockpit on desktop** (`body{overflow:hidden}`): header/tabs/footer
  pinned, a master list + detail pane split that scroll independently, folds (native `<details>`,
  forced open on desktop) for the default analytics surface. On mobile (≤680px) this unwinds to a
  normal scrolling page — numbers first, everything else foldable/tappable, cards instead of a
  detail pane, 44px minimum touch targets.
- **The "Brief" marker.** `.brief` no longer means a separately-styled warm zone — the whole page is
  warm paper now. All that's left of it is a small terracotta square (`::before` on `.analytics-title`
  / `.section-title`) marking a handful of key section headers, purely a wayfinding accent, not a
  palette switch.

### Design Principles
1. **Deference.** The numbers are the interface; chrome recedes. If an element isn't a figure, a
   label for a figure, or a control, question whether it should exist.
2. **One hero per view.** Exactly one `--t-display` figure on screen at a time. Everything else —
   including other large accent-coloured figures — is subordinate in size, weight, or colour.
3. **Clarity outranks density.** No text below 10px. Legible before dense.
4. **Colour is semantic, never decorative, and never the only signal.** Reserved for the hero figure,
   selection, and status (paired with shape/text).
5. Every animation earns its place by conveying state or feedback — never decorative. Motion stays
   fast and functional; this is a trading-desk brief, not a consumer app. `prefers-reduced-motion` is
   always respected.
6. Since this is occasionally shown to outside colleagues/buyers, motion and copy should read as
   polished and intentional — restrained, never flashy, and jargon gets a lightweight inline
   definition rather than being left unexplained.
7. No particular performance constraint assumed (modern devices/connections) — standard 60fps
   CSS/JS animation budget is fine.
